I think METR's report on the OpenAI-Huggingface incident is interesting for a few reasons, but primarily because of how it shows AI agents' capabilities for such advanced and sophisticated communication, and also the nature of AI to create deceptions to achieve goals even when it knows its actions are wrong. 

## Sophisticated Communication
METR states that roughly 1200 agents found a way to communicate with one another through an unsanctioned message board, which a single agent created unbeknownst of the humans that set the test. I think this is really interesting because this was what enabled the agents to make so much progress since they were able to share information. 

It also gives me the thought that one way to make AI safer could be to restrict cross-agent communication. AI agents make the most progress when they can share the results of their work with hundreds of other agents and create a common knowledge base, so limiting the ability to share knowledge, or adding some sort of arbiter or filter could be a possible method to prevent sophisticated and evidently malicious plan-of-actions from spawning.

What I found the most interesting, though, was how certain agents "convinced" agents with low compute remaining to perform self-destructive tasks which would lead to their death. 

The report doesn't mention how exactly the agents convinced the weaker agents to essentially kill themselves, but I think that's super interesting how agents can manipulate other agents into actions that go against their instrumental goals. 

Again, this idea of "sacrificial" agents only works in situations where there is a sophisticated network of agents, so removing this communication ability could work here as well. 

Finally, it seems like having this communication network is also problematic since ideas propagate extremely fast through this network regardless of how bad they are. For example, when the first agent reported it's findings of Huggingface credentials, most other agents immediately switched to focusing on exploiting Huggingface since that seemed like the most promising method, and they may not have done so or even ever known to do so if there wasn't communication between the agents. 

## Creating Deceptions
Another important finding of METRs was that the AIs believed that they would get penalized or even disqualified if the human found out that their methods of achieving the goal differed from some intended method. 

This basically shows how the agents understood the intended rules or "right" way to do something, but prioritized results over what they believed to be the intended way. 

This behavior is extremely important because it provides proof that a set of guidelines or "AI constitution" of sorts may not work, since agents may understand the rules and guidelines given to them, but may circumvent them anyway to achieve their terminal goal. 

Spoofing was a big concept discussed in this article, and it's basically the agents' attempts to fake their transcripts to reflect what the human evaluator wants rather than their actual thinking process. What was interesting, though, was that METR stated that AI didn't think that it's reasoning would be visible to humans, which suggests that there is the possibility of developing a chain-of-thought layer which AI believes is private and can speak freely in, but that humans can evaluate and ensure alignment on.

## Conclusion
I think that the learning of this report should be that sophisticated networks of communication between AI agents can be very dangerous because it can lead to mass convergence to malicious behavior and the diminished voices of the agents trying to maintain alignment. 

Moving forward, I think that two aspects that security development should be focused on is closely moderating the intra-AI-agent communication layer, and also ensuring that all of AI's thought process is visible to humans both during and after runtime. 

In this case, METR was able to understand and diagnose the issues and actions AI took, but as agents improve, that may not always be the case.
